# How to show Google reviews on your website

Finding you on Google and trusting your website are two different steps. Plenty of people open the site after they have seen the listing. If the reviews stay only on Google, that page does not show the same proof.

## Use a widget, not a screenshot

A screenshot of your Google reviews goes stale the day a new one is published. A Google review widget on the site shows the reviews that are already on your Google Business Profile, and it can update when a new review comes in. You should not have to edit the page each time.

The widget is not a place to type your own quotes. It should show the Google reviews you actually have. Do not invent a star score or a review count to sit next to it.

## What has to be in place first

You need the Google Business Profile for that location, and access to the website. The widget has to be tied to the right profile, or you will show the wrong business.

If someone else built the site, they may need to add a small embed. That is a small website fix, not a new website. On the ReviewBuddy plan, a small website fix is $99 incl. GST if you need one.

## Where to put it

The homepage is the usual place, because that is where an enquiry starts. A plumbing, electrical, or building page can use the same widget if that is the page people land on from Google.

Put it where it can be read before someone calls. Do not bury it on a page nobody opens.

## The link and the QR code do a different job

The widget shows reviews that already exist. The Google review link, and the QR code that opens it, are how the next customer leaves one. Use both.

The link goes in the text or the email after the job. The QR code goes on the invoice, the van, or the counter. Ask every customer. Google’s policies do not allow you to send only the happy ones to the public review page.
